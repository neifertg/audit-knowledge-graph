Audit knowledge graph

Audit risk hides between the tables. This is what it looks like when you connect them.

A synthetic US regional bank keeps its risks in the ERM register and in Internal Audit's risk and control matrices. They describe the same business in different words. Reconciled into a single framework and connected as a graph, they show exposure no table holds on its own.

Synthetic data throughout; descriptions AI-written ahead of time and committed; no real institution, person or vendor.

Does a graph beat SQL?

Asked {{derived:rounds_count|count_word}} planning questions in both, with the same facts and answers proved equal: SQL wins {{derived:verdict_counts.SQL|times_word}}, the graph {{derived:verdict_counts.graph|times_word}}, and {{derived:verdict_counts.close|count_word}} are close. The difference is how much query each needs, not speed: no speed claims are made at demo scale. Details below.

The graph informs auditor judgment; it does not score risk or set the audit plan.

The rounds

Each planning question was asked in SQL and in Cypher over the same facts. Each answer is checked: the SQL and Cypher answers must agree, an independent Python computation must agree, and where a scenario was planted, the answer must find it. The verdict compares how much query each language needs; it is not a measure of speed. Rounds {{derived:near_the_line|names_and}} sit within a point of the line, so a single extra clause would change their verdict.

How the verdict is measured

Each side scores clauses plus steps plus {{rounds.json:verdict_rule.weight}} times its moving parts; the side whose score times {{rounds.json:verdict_rule.margin}} is at most the other's wins, and otherwise the round is close. Moving parts follow a single rule on both sides, with {{derived:disclosed_count|count_word}} disclosed exceptions; clauses and steps measure what each language writes.

When a graph earns its place

What the rounds measured

Cypher needed less query in rounds {{derived:graph_rule|names_and}}: with the same number of stages on both sides, SQL needed more joins than Cypher needed hops, to fetch a name or to join a grouped result back. The languages came out close in rounds {{derived:close_steps|names_and}}, where SQL's joins were no more than Cypher's hops, and in round {{derived:close_moving|names_and}}, where Cypher needed an extra stage. SQL needed less query in round {{derived:rounds_by_verdict.SQL|names_and}}, the question of exposure through fourth parties, of unknown depth: asked on its own, the question was shorter in Cypher, {{site.json:round5_split.query.cypher}} against {{site.json:round5_split.query.sql}}, but bringing the new register in cost the graph {{site.json:round5_split.setup.cypher}} against SQL's {{site.json:round5_split.setup.sql}}, because a new table had to become nodes and edges.

Where a graph should earn its place

expected, not measured in M1 When auditors explore rather than report, as in clicking through a blast radius; when the same entities are linked from many systems; when new relationship types keep arriving and the data is already in the graph; and at larger scale, which is the M2 benchmark's question.

When good SQL views are enough

The measured rounds say: when the joins a question needs are no more than the hops, and when new data arrives as tables.

expected, not measured in M1 Most audit and data teams already read SQL, which matters as much as query length.

Keys for success

practice, from building this project Reconcile first: a graph of unreconciled data only draws the confusion more vividly. Keep the graph an analysis layer over the system of record, not a replacement. Prove both stores hold the same facts on every load. Keep a human review queue for uncertain matches. Publish every answer with its checks.

Tests and CI

Every round's answer must agree between the engines, match an independent Python computation, and find its planted scenario where a scenario was planted. The full suite runs against a real graph database on every push: the test runs.

Limitations

The data is synthetic, from a single generator and a single bank, with small samples. The matcher's thresholds are deliberately conservative, so most true matches wait in the review queue. The training bank comes from the same generator, so its weights know the generator's habits. No speed or scale claim is made. The graph informs auditor judgment; it does not score risk or set the plan.
