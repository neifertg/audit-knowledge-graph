VERDICT: REVISE

Context checked: the current full build (`akg build --size full --seed 42`, written to a temp folder) and the sample build were measured with a throwaway script; generate.py, the epic, the existing tests and the ROADMAP parked notes were read. Full build today: 81 auditable entities, of which **66 have any IA risk exposure (15 have none)**. 150 IA risks: 50 High. 37 entities have at least one High IA risk (30 of them exactly one; only 7 have 2-4). Each IA risk has 2-3 IA controls. 15 regulations, and one of them reaches no business unit.

1. [major] AC-3's coverage band and AC-4(b) do not fit together under the audit design in "Data impact". The 15 entities with no IA risk can never be audited meaningfully, because an audit "tests only IA controls mitigating risks those entities are exposed to". They alone make 15/81 = 18.5% unaudited, so "10-25% of auditable entities are never audited" leaves room for at most 5 more unaudited entities (20/81 = 24.7%). Data impact says audits test "the IA controls (specific and generic) of the risks those entities are exposed to", which reads as all of them. If so, a High IA risk goes untested only when its entity is never audited. Getting "at least 8 High-rated IA risks none of whose IA controls any audit tested" from at most 5 entities then forces you to leave unaudited the entities with 3-4 High risks each, the riskiest in the bank. That defeats the "risk-based" story AC-3 asks for. Also, 75 audits x 1-3 entities over 66 candidates covers nearly every candidate by chance, so the 10-25% band is decided by structure rather than by the audit plan. -> State how untested High risks arise. Realistic options are partial scoping (an audit tests only some of its entities' risks or controls) or unaudited entities chosen on purpose. Then redo the arithmetic against the real counts above. Measure the coverage gap over entities that have IA exposure, or say explicitly that the 15 non-IA entities count toward it. Require every audit to test at least one control.

2. [major] AC-3's "entities with at least one High IA risk average more audits than entities without" cannot fail. The "without" group includes the 15 entities with no IA risk, and they have 0 audits by construction. That pulls the group's average down, so even a uniform, non-risk-based sampler passes. The comparison also has no margin, so noise alone could pass it. -> Compare only among entities with IA exposure, and set a margin (for example, a ratio of at least 1.3). Check the margin once at the checkpoint against a uniform sampler so you know the test can fail.

3. [major] AC-4(d) fails on today's data, and no slice fixes it. In the full build, "Fair Credit Reporting Act" (REG-756) is required by no control in either source. `library.py` names it only in the regulation-citation table, and no theme's control carries it. The plan says "(checked, not listed)" as if the condition already holds. The fix is a library change (some full-only theme's control gets FCRA). It must not touch `RISK_CONCEPTS` themes that the sample uses. It changes only the `regulation_requires_control_*` files, so recheck 008's shared-regulation F1 (< 0.5) afterwards. Note that the sample has the same gap: "Regulation E" (REG-787) reaches no business unit, and the sample is frozen. -> Add the fix to slice 3 with its constraints. State that (d) is full-size only and that the sample's gap stays.

4. [major] AC-4(c) can pass without planting anything. "rises at least 50% from 2023 to 2025" has no minimum base, and the scenario names whichever L1 qualifies. True-L1 sizes are uneven: IA risks by true ERM L1 are Market 4, Strategic 5, Liquidity 7, Reputational 7. With about 83 issues a year, a small category moving from 2 to 3 issues qualifies, and with 7 categories some small one will often qualify by noise. A wrong (unplanted) implementation can therefore pass. -> Require a floor, for example at least 10 issues in 2023 and a rise of at least N issues in absolute terms. Also define the year as the year of `opened_date`, for (a) as well.

5. [minor] As written, AC-5 cannot pass. The ER diagram contains CANONICAL_RISK, CANONICAL_CONTROL, `maps_to` and `ia_maps_to_erm`, which are E2 output; the epic says E1 writes them only to the answer key. The criterion also does not say how the test enumerates "every type". A hard-coded list (like test_sample_build's) cannot catch an edge someone forgets. -> Parse the mermaid block in docs/data-model.md and use an explicit E2 exemption list, so a new diagram edge with no rows fails the test.

6. [minor] AC-6's `test_ac6_issue_text_keys_are_natural` has no stated observable. Text keys are never published, and "never positional" is a property of the implementation. A key such as `issue:IA:<anything>:<j>` would pass a format check. Note also that "control instance key" is the wrong term: ERM generic controls are keyed `theme#kind` and IA generic controls `instance#kind`. -> Specify that the test (i) asserts keys are unique at both sizes, and (ii) resolves each key's source and control part against that issue's published `source` and `issue_against_control_*` row, so a positional or mislinked key fails. Say "control key", not "control instance key".

7. [minor] AC-7 can pass even if the override does nothing. If `--count PERSON=100` were ignored at full size, every file would be identical and the test would pass. -> Also assert that person.csv has 100 rows and that every `issue_owned_by_person` person_id exists in it. That mirrors the existing ac11 test's "override must differ" guard.

8. [minor] The default-size flip has unlisted fallout. Name each case, or a reviewer cannot tell whether "make sample explicit in helpers" covered it:
