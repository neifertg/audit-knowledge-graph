VERDICT: REVISE
1. [major] The orphan rule produces wrong truth. The plan says orphans are "specific controls kept for risk instances that source no longer holds" and "orphans sit in no cluster". In the seed-42 full build, the risk answer key has 90 shared instances, 77 ERM-only, 60 IA-only and 13 duplicates, so ERM holds 167 of the 232 instances and IA holds 150. Only 232 - (77 + 60 + 90) = 5 instances are held by neither source. AC-1 needs at least 3% orphans in each source: about 9 for ERM (of ~292-300) and about 12 for IA (of ~390). So at least 16 orphan controls must come from instances the other source holds, and the other source already has that instance's specific control. An IA orphan for an ERM-only instance X is the same control as ERM's specific control for X, and would map to the same canonical control, but the key labels the pair unmatched. A correct E2 matcher then loses precision on every one of these pairs. AC-4 counts them as non-matches (TF-IDF's top match for such an orphan is its twin), and they also inflate the hard-negative share. -> Choose orphans so that no control in the other source shares their concept. One option is to take an orphan from the 5 unheld instances. Another is to make an orphan a generic control of a (theme, kind) that the other source never uses: an IA kind outside ERM's kinds for that theme, or an ERM (theme, kind) with no IA control of that kind. A third is to put twins in their twin's cluster and cap the "matched control with no risk" cue in AC-5. Add the check to AC-1: no orphan shares an instance, or a (theme, kind), with any control of the other source.
2. [minor] The overlap blocker is fixed, and the bands fit together. I simulated it against the seed-42 risk selection: 56 ERM themes, ERM kinds per theme drawn from {1,2,2,3} (2 on average), 1-2 IA generics per IA risk with distinct kinds, a skewed 8-kind mix, and 4.5% orphans. Results:
   - Counts: ERM is about 292 and IA about 390, both inside the ±10% bands.
   - Overlap by dial probability: 0.5 gives a median of 57.0% (5-95% range 53.9-59.8%); 0.6 gives 60.6% (57.9-63.8%); 0.7 gives 64.5% (61.3-67.0%).
   - So 55-65% is reachable at a dial of about 0.55-0.65. The margin is only about ±3 points, so a single seed can land outside the band. AC-5 (g) is comfortable: the ERM mean-count difference has a median of 0.06, a 90th percentile of 0.15, and P(>=0.3) of 0.001. IA counts are drawn per instance and independently, so they are safer still.
   -> At the checkpoint, set the dial near the middle of the band (about 0.6), not near an edge. Record the chosen value and the measured overlap in the plan's checkpoint note.
3. [minor] The AC-2 shared-system floor almost cannot fail, and the top 3 may be set by the tie-break. Today's full build has only one control per concept, and its systems already reach 54, 45, 43, 28, 27 ... of 81 entities (union over both sources, 23 systems with any reach). Most systems already pass "at least 3 entities in at least 2 business units". Bank-wide ERM generics reach a median of 9 and up to 21 entities each (seed-42 ERM exposure per theme). Any system attached to them will push reach toward saturation, and saturated reach makes ties at rank 3 likely, so the result is ordered by name. The plan says only "ERM lists systems for only some controls", and does not say which kinds carry systems. -> State which generic kinds may depend on a system (for example: never policy, training or management review). Add an observable condition to AC-2: no tie at rank 3, meaning the third system's reach strictly exceeds the fourth's. This keeps the scenario a real ranking, not a sort by name.
4. [minor] No baseline caps the subject-plus-kind lookup. About 145 of the roughly 235 matched IA controls (about 60%) are generic. Each matched IA generic has exactly one possible ERM partner, the (theme, kind) control, and both texts name the theme subject. Since each source's kind wording comes from a fixed 8-kind vocabulary, a keyword rule (subject phrase plus kind keyword) may solve most generic pairs exactly. AC-4 caps only bag-of-words TF-IDF. Real generic pairs are often easy, so this is not a leak in itself. But if the rule is near perfect, the published control precision and recall (O3) mostly measure a lookup. -> Measure a subject-plus-kind rule baseline at the checkpoint and report it. Alternatively, add an AC-5 (h) cap. A third option is to vary the ERM generic wording, such as combined kinds ("policy and training"), so that the kind is not a clean token.
